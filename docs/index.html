The Core Sociological Paradox

When technological progress accelerates, society does not evaluate its condition based on cumulative achievements \(R(t)\), but on the psychological derivative \(E(t)\). As normalized velocity \(s(t)\) passes the threshold \(s^* = \left(\frac{\beta}{2\beta+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta+1}}\), expectations begin a steep crash even while actual production capacity continues to climb. This mathematically explains why societies undergoing the most rapid technological revolutions can simultaneously experience profound existential cynicism, burnout, and generational exhaustion.

The Epistemic Boundary

The rationality of a belief as a practical choice is not the same as the truth of that belief. Practical rationality evaluates utility; epistemic rationality demands truth. Believing in an overarching witness allows an individual to resist the nihilism of historical lottery without abandoning empirical rigor in science and technology.

The Tension Between Realism and Outlier Will

"Realism can preserve your life, and realism can pave your tomb; idealism built civilizations, and ungrounded idealism manufactures psychosis."
